Defeating Dark-Web Infostealers (Lumma Stealer, AMOS), Clipboard Hijackers & Hardware Wallet Address Leaks with 2-Factor Paper Cold Storage

Executive Takeaway: Dark-web threat reports identify three primary vectors draining self-custodial users: (1) browser-extension vault stealers (Lumma Stealer, Atomic macOS Stealer / AMOS), (2) clipboard 0x address hijackers (ClipBanker), and (3) e-commerce residential shipping address leaks from hardware wallet orders. CryptoSLIP V3.2 neutralizes all three via client-side 2-Factor Paper Cold Vaults (100,000-round PBKDF2/scrypt + permanent No take-back) and reversible bearer chat links.

1. Threat Vector A: Browser-Extension Vault Infostealers (Lumma & AMOS)

Commodity malware families such as Lumma Stealer (Windows) and Atomic macOS Stealer (AMOS) target hot-wallet browser extensions by copying the raw LevelDB files from Local Extension Settings. Even password-protected extension vaults are subjected to offline GPU brute-forcing once exfiltrated.

There is 0 bytes of vault data on disk for an infostealer to harvest.

2. Threat Vector B: Clipboard 0x Address Swappers (ClipBanker)

Clipboard hijackers continuously regex-match the operating system clipboard for ^0x[a-fA-F0-9]{40}$. When a user copies a recipient's 0x wallet address from Telegram, Discord, or WhatsApp, the trojan silently swaps it with an attacker-controlled address before the user clicks Send.

  • No 0x Copy-Pasting in Chat: CryptoSLIP replaces raw 0x address requests with 1:1 asset-backed bearer links (#k=... per RFC 3986 § 3.5).
  • 1-Tap Take back (refundSlip at 0% Fee): If a sender pastes a payment link into the wrong chat or suspects interception prior to claim, tapping Take back immediately refunds 100% of the tokens to the creator's wallet.

3. Threat Vector C: Hardware Wallet E-Commerce PII Leaks & Physical Extortion

Purchasing a physical USB or Bluetooth hardware wallet requires providing your legal name, email address, phone number, and residential street address to an e-commerce store and shipping courier. Historical breaches leaked over 270,000+ physical home addresses of hardware-wallet buyers onto dark-web forums, enabling SIM-swap attacks and physical home-invasion (“$5 wrench”) extortion.

CryptoSLIP Paper Cold Storage requires zero shipping address, zero email, zero phone number, zero USB firmware, and zero vendor database. Any printer and two sheets of paper create an institutional-grade 2-factor cold vault on Base (8453).
