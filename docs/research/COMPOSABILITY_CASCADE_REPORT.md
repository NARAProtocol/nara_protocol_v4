```
NARA PROTOCOL: COMPOSABILITY CASCADE REPORT v2
```

**One-sentence description:**
NARA is a fixed-supply, sealed-reserve, epoch-based NARA-locking and ETH-routing yield primitive where time preference is priced on-chain, every ETH-generating product can route value to lockers, and locked positions are becoming tradeable collateral.

**Top 5 "Holy Shit" Insights:**

1. **The float trap is structural, not temporary.** The lockboard demands 31,250 NARA to fill. The liquid float is ~14,498 NARA. This is not a coincidence  it's a designed coordination bottleneck that creates buy pressure regardless of board outcomes.

2. **Bond opening is a predictable ETH velocity spike.** When bonds open, a large single epoch will receive concentrated ETH via notifyEthRewards(). Lockers active in that epoch capture a windfall. Anyone who watches on-chain signals for bond activation can time their lock to be active.

3. **Bootstrap decay creates a known yield step function.** As bootstrapWeight decays toward zero, real lockers capture a larger share of each epoch's emission without locking more NARA. It's a built-in yield improvement schedule visible to anyone who can read the contract.

4. **The SponsorHub inverts the "donation" model.** Sponsors don't give NARA to prizes. They lock NARA in the engine, earn yield, and that yield funds the prizes. Their principal is recoverable. This is more like a yield-bearing deposit with prize routing than a donation  a completely new DeFi primitive.

5. **The 90,000 protocol-weight bloc is not self-reinforcing.** `lock()` stored 60,000 + 30,000 weight once; it does not auto-grow. Both positions were created at the full `35,040`-epoch max, so `extend()` cannot increase them. If outsiders keep re-locking claimed NARA while the protocol stays static, protocol share drifts down by design even before the final unlock event.

**Innovation Rating: 8.5/10**

**Verdict:** Not a memecoin with staking. A genuinely novel yield architecture with three distinct phase transitions ahead (bond opening, bootstrap expiry, protocol-position expiry) and a composability surface that third parties haven't touched yet. The long-term ETH-yield thesis is real, but it is not literally an ETH staking platform today because users lock NARA, not ETH.

## CORE MECHANIC: STATIC WEIGHT, DYNAMIC SHARE

- `lock()` stores a fixed `amount` and fixed `weight`, then schedules activation and deactivation. It is not a rebasing position.
- `extend()` recalculates weight for the same `p.amount` only. It cannot add claimed NARA into an existing lock.
- `claimRewards()` transfers accrued rewards out to the wallet. NARA claims have no fee. ETH claims pay `claimFeeBps = 500`. Claims do not change stored position weight.
- Live snapshot: protocol long locks are `20,000 NARA -> 60,000 weight` and `10,000 NARA -> 30,000 weight`, for `90,000 / 103,284 = 87.14%` of active weight.
- Both protocol positions are already full-length locks: `unlockEpoch - createdEpoch - 1 = 35,040`, which equals `maxLockEpochs`. Calling `extend()` on them now would revert `LockTooLong()`.
- At current config, a max-duration re-lock gives a `3x` multiplier. After the 2% lock fee, every `1 NARA` claimed and re-locked becomes `2.94` new weight.
- Live emission is `50.6224961321 NARA/day`. At current shares, the protocol earns `44.1116208889 NARA/day` and everyone else earns `6.5108752432 NARA/day`.
- If the protocol opens no fresh locks while outsiders re-lock all claimed NARA, outsiders add about `19.1419732150` weight/day and protocol stays static at `90,000`.
- Under that simple live-state path, protocol share falls from `87.14%` to about `85%` in `126` days, `80%` in `381` days, and `75%` in `606` days.
- By the treasury unlock in about `337.25` days, protocol share is roughly `80.92%` if protocol re-locks none of its emissions, `84.54%` if it re-locks half, and flat at `87.14%` only if it re-locks all of them into fresh positions.
- This is the protective design: the big genesis locks keep their stored weight until unlock, but they do not entrench forever. Relative dominance decays unless it is actively maintained.

##  COMPOSABILITY OPPORTUNITIES (Phase 3)

### Internal (Protocol-Only)

**1. Claim-and-Relock Relay**
- Primitives: claim() + lock() + notifyEthRewards() routing
- How it works: Script claims NARA rewards and opens fresh max-duration locks. If it also harvests ETH, it can route that ETH after accounting for the 5% ETH claim fee.
- Value proposition: The edge is not magical compounding inside one position. The edge is preserving or growing share while static positions stay flat. NARA claims are fee-free; only ETH claims pay 5%.
- Impact:  HIGH  Every locker who keeps re-locking emissions slowly gains relative weight against lockers who leave big positions static.

**2. Claim-Then-Bond Relay**
- Primitives: claim() + bond purchase flow
- How it works: Claim NARA rewards, rotate some value into ETH, then buy a bond to receive a discounted locked position NFT.
- Value proposition: Converts earned value into a potentially larger locked position via the bond discount once the bond market opens.
- Impact:  MEDIUM-HIGH  Only viable after bonds open.

**3. Bootstrap Decay Timing Strategy**
- Primitives: bootstrapWeight state + lock timing
- How it works: Calculate exactly when bootstrapWeight decays below meaningful threshold; lock before that point to capture the yield step-up
- Value proposition: Predictable yield improvement schedule is visible to anyone who reads epochState
- Impact:  HIGH  Asymmetric information advantage for on-chain readers.

**4. Multi-Position Weight Distribution**
- Primitives: 64-position limit + lock() multiple
- How it works: Spread across multiple positions with different durations and unlock epochs
- Value proposition: Liquidity laddering  stagger unlock epochs so NARA returns at intervals
- Impact:  MEDIUM  Basic portfolio management tool.

**5. Lockboard Slot Meta-Strategy**
- Primitives: lockboard claim rules + max lock requirement + liquid float scarcity
- How it works: Float is ~14,498 NARA. Lockboard needs 31,250 to fill. Anyone who controls a large float position controls the slot meta.
- Value proposition: Early float buyers can strategically time slot claims.
- Impact:  HIGH  The float trap is a real coordination game.

**6. ETH Spike Timing**
- Primitives: notifyEthRewards() + epoch advance timing + lock activation
- How it works: Monitor pendingEthForNextEpoch. Ensure your lock is active before epoch advance. Capture full spike.
- Value proposition: ETH distributes fully in one epoch. Late activations miss the spike entirely.
- Impact:  HIGH  No smoothing = timing matters enormously.

**7. Arena Burn Rank Identity**
- Primitives: BurnRunArenaV3 burn ranks (1K/10K/100K/1M/10M NARA)
- How it works: Lifetime burns accumulate to rank. Rank gives action slot bonuses.
- Value proposition: High-rank players get structural advantages in every future arena session.
- Impact:  MEDIUM  First movers in arena accumulate permanent rank advantage.

**8. Lotto Fixed-Seat Arbitrage (LottoV3)**
- Primitives: LottoV3 tier multipliers + seat count odds
- How it works: Higher lock duration tier = higher ticket multiplier per seat. Optimize tier selection vs. seat cost.
- Value proposition: Tier 3 seats cost more NARA but have outsized multiplier vs. Tier 1. Seat-count cap creates ceiling.
- Impact:  MEDIUM  Tier selection is a non-obvious optimization in V3 model.

**9. Sponsor-as-Protocol Revenue Stream**
- Primitives: NARASponsorHub + engine yield
- How it works: Deploy hub, create campaigns, let external protocols sponsor prize pools for branding
- Value proposition: Charge protocols to sponsor games. Protocol earns sponsorship fees without diluting supply.
- Impact:  VERY HIGH  This is the primitives for B2B protocol revenue.

**10. Keeper Arbitrage Window**
- Primitives: epoch backlog + ETH spike timing
- How it works: If keeper misses an epoch, backlog accumulates. Call advanceEpochs() when pendingEth is large.
- Value proposition: Anyone can advance epochs. Anyone who advances at the right moment captures a favorable distribution for their position.
- Impact:  LOW-MEDIUM  Permissionless but requires active monitoring.

**11. Position Extension Strategy**
- Primitives: extend() function
- How it works: Extend a near-maturity position to avoid the unlock gap. Weight is recomputed from current config for the same principal only.
- Value proposition: Useful for under-max-duration positions. It does not compound claimed NARA into the same lock.
- Impact:  MEDIUM  Not available to the current treasury/owner long locks because they already sit at `35,040` epochs.

**12. Selective Unlock Timing**
- Primitives: unlock() requiring epochState.epoch >= unlockEpoch
- How it works: Unlock immediately after reaching unlockEpoch vs. waiting longer
- Value proposition: Operational flexibility only. Rewards stop accruing once epoch >= unlockEpoch, so waiting does not create free extra yield.
- Impact:  LOW  Mostly a liquidity-timing choice, not an alpha source.

**13. Weight Recalculation Arbitrage on Config Change**
- Primitives: extend() + pending config changes
- How it works: Config changes are staged (propose  delay  execute). Before config changes lower the multiplier, extend positions to lock in old (better) weight.
- Value proposition: Pre-config-change extensions preserve higher weight against future lowering.
- Impact:  MEDIUM  Requires reading the governance queue.

**14. MisterMint Legacy Distribution Claiming**

- How it works: Protocol distributes NARA/ETH to legacy Mister Mint holders via this contract
- Value proposition: Legacy holders who claim early and lock immediately into the engine capture bootstrap-era yield advantages

##  EMERGENT BEHAVIORS (Phase 4)

### Game Theory

```
Game Theory:

 Nash Equilibrium: Max-duration locking at high capital
   Why stable: The weight formula gives a 3 multiplier at max duration. Any locker who
     uses a shorter duration is permanently weight-disadvantaged against max lockers.
     Everyone converges to max lock as the dominant rational strategy  unless they need liquidity.

 Dominant Strategy: Lock max duration immediately, claim lazily (5% fee on ETH claims)
   Proof: Weight is fixed at lock time. Locking now vs. later costs the same NARA but gives
     more epoch rewards. Claiming ETH has a 5% fee. Claim infrequently in large batches to minimize fee drag.

 Coordination Game: Lockboard slot race
   Mechanism: Only 100 slots. Max-lock requirement is non-negotiable. Float is scarce.
     First wallets to acquire float AND lock at max duration get the founding slots.
     Coordination failure = slots go to whoever acquires float fastest, not who deserves them "most."

 Prisoner's Dilemma: Keeper operation
    Mechanism: Any wallet can advance epochs. But it costs gas. Everyone benefits from advancement.
      Everyone wants someone else to pay. If Railway cron fails, who calls advanceEpochs() deg 
      Lockers with the most to lose (high ETH in queue) have the strongest incentive to self-rescue.
```

### Economic Dynamics

```
Economics:

 Spontaneous Markets:
   Lock NFT secondary market (NaraLockNFT on OpenSea)
     Why: Locked NARA becomes liquid via NFT transfer. Price = discounted present value of future yield.
   NARA itself at thin float
     Why: Demand (lockboard 31,250 NARA) > supply (float 14,498 NARA). Organic price pressure.
   "Slot Rights" informal market
      Why: Someone who locked and claimed a slot can transfer both the slot record AND the underlying NFT.
        That slot + identity + position = a bundle worth more than its parts.
```

##  THE RABBIT HOLE (Phase 10)

**Non-Obvious Insight #1:**
Stored weight does not self-compound. This is the part almost everyone misses.

 Why this matters: The giant launch positions are not permanent auto-entrenchment. Their stored `90,000` weight stays flat until unlock, while smaller lockers can slowly build fresh weight by re-locking claimed NARA.
 Hidden because: People conflate `extend()` with compounding and miss that both protocol locks are already at the `35,040`-epoch max.
 Proof: `lock()` stores fixed `amount` and `weight`; `claimRewards()` transfers rewards without touching the position; `extend()` recomputes weight for the same `p.amount` and reverts above `maxLockEpochs`.
 Real numbers: At today's `103,284` active weight, the protocol sits at `87.14%`. If it opens no fresh locks while outsiders re-lock all emissions, protocol share drifts to ~`85%` in `126` days and ~`80%` in `381` days.

**Non-Obvious Insight #2:**
The liquid float (14,498 NARA) is structurally smaller than lockboard demand (31,250 NARA). This cannot resolve without either someone selling their locked position NFT or the protocol cycling earned NARA back into circulation.

 Why this matters: The coordination bottleneck is mathematical. Even if 100 wallets want to fill the board, there isn't enough float to buy NARA for all 100.
 Hidden because: The lockboard shows 100 empty slots. It doesn't show the float constraint.
 Proof: 31,250 NARA needed. 14,498 liquid. Gap = 16,752 NARA must come from: protocol recycling earned NARA, bond vault at discount, or NFT position sales.
 Enables: Strategic pre-positioning of float acquisition before lockboard awareness grows.

**Non-Obvious Insight #3:**
Bootstrap weight creates a "phantom locker" that steals yield from real lockers in early epochs  and it's already mostly done, but the schedule is readable.

 Why this matters: At epoch 2999, bootstrapWeight has been decaying since genesis. The remaining phantom dilution is computable. Every future epoch, real lockers capture slightly more yield.
 Hidden because: It's a contract parameter, not a visible UI element.
 Proof: `bootstrapWeight[n+1] = bootstrapWeight[n]  bootstrapDecayWad / WAD`. It's monotonically shrinking.
 Enables: Calculating the exact epoch at which bootstrapWeight drops below 1% of total effective weight  a threshold event that meaningfully increases yield per unit of real weight.

**Non-Obvious Insight #4:**
The bond system has a fixed ceiling that cannot be refilled beyond 250,000 NARA total lifetime. Even with earned NARA recycled in, cumulative releases cannot exceed `initialBondAllocation`.

 Why this matters: Every bond that opens at a bad price is permanently wasted from the ceiling. The bond vault is a one-way non-renewable resource.
 Conventional wisdom: "You can refill the bond vault with earned NARA." True. But refilling doesn't expand the ceiling. Total distributed bonds  250K NARA, forever.
 Proof: `proposeReleaseCap(newCap)` reverts if `newCap > initialBondAllocation`.
 Implications: Bond opening is a capital allocation decision, not just a market event. Each poorly-timed open is irreversible.

**Non-Obvious Insight #5:**
ETH queued in pendingEthForNextEpoch distributes fully in a SINGLE epoch advance. If multiple epoch advances happen in sequence (catching up backlog), ETH is not split between them  it distributes in the first advance that clears it.

 Why this matters: A large ETH input (e.g., Arena launch) concentrated in one epoch creates a one-time windfall for that epoch's lockers. Time your activation precisely.
 Hidden because: Nothing in the UI shows this. Users think "I'll earn my share over time." No  you earn or miss it in one epoch.
 Proof: `distEth = pendingEthForNextEpoch`  the full pending amount, distributed once, then reset.
 Enables: ETH spike timing as a high-alpha strategy. Monitor the on-chain pending balance.

**Non-Obvious Insight #6:**
The SponsorHub architecture means a sponsor can retarget their yield to a different game mid-lock without moving their principal. This creates an entirely new category: "yield-directed sponsorships" that are competitive with each other.

 Why this matters: If multiple games compete for sponsor yield, games must offer value to sponsors to attract their retargeting. This creates a B2B competitive dynamic within the protocol ecosystem.
 Hidden because: The sponsor architecture isn't live yet and the retargeting mechanic is buried in contract logic.
 Implications: Games will eventually need to attract not just players but sponsor yield  creating a two-sided market inside NARA.

**Counterintuitive Truth #1:**
Locking less NARA for longer is structurally better than locking more NARA for shorter  when comparing by total expected yield over the same calendar period.

 Conventional wisdom: Bigger lock = more yield.
 Reality: Weight = netAmount  multiplier. At max duration (3), 100 NARA = 300 weight. At half duration (1.75), 171 NARA is needed to match that weight. After unlock and re-lock, the second locker has paid more fees and missed more epochs.
 Proof: The compounding of max-duration weight advantage over short-duration repeated locks dominates when accounting for 2% lock fee on each re-lock, 5% claim fee on ETH, and the activation delay gap on each new lock.
 Implications: The max-lock dominant strategy is mathematically rigorous, not just intuitive.

**Counterintuitive Truth #2:**
The protocol having a dominant share of weight is NOT automatically bad for public lockers  it's good. More total weight means higher WLS means higher total emission, which grows the pie for everyone.

 Conventional wisdom: "Protocol controls too much of emissions  that's centralized and bad."
 Reality: WLS = activeWeight / (circulatingSupply + activeWeight + bootstrapWeight). More real locked weight = higher WLS = higher emission factor. The protocol's weight is additive to the total emission cake. Public lockers get a smaller *slice* of a bigger *pie*.
 Proof: At WLS near 0, emission factor is near 1. At WLS = 1, emission factor is 1 + a (incentive bonus). More weight = bigger cake.
 Implications: The team's dominant position can benefit early public lockers more than harms them, even if the optics are still centralizing.
